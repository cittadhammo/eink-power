How much is the e-paper screen costing this laptop?

A Modos Paper Dev Kit 13″ (Carta 1000, 1600×1200@75 Hz) on a Framework Laptop 13, AMD Ryzen 5 7640U, Omarchy, kernel 7.2.5-3-omarchy. Measured 2026-09-27.

The answer

1.4 W

That is what the display draws out of the battery, continuously, to hold a still image.

The kit takes video and power through a single USB-C cable, so pulling that cable removes the entire e-paper subsystem — panel, controller board, FPGA, and the GPU’s display pipeline — in one move. The difference in battery draw between the plugged and unplugged states is the display’s cost, with no modelling required.

The run

phase draw vs connected
monitor connected 6.66 W —
monitor unplugged 5.22 W −1.44 W
plugged back in 6.75 W +0.09 W

Three independent estimators agree: 1.44 W (settled mean), 1.38 W (median), 1.50 W (quietest 5% of each phase). The connected phase held a standard deviation of 0.04 W over 65 s, so the machine was quiet enough to resolve a 1.4 W effect, and the third phase plugged the monitor back in and returned the draw to within 0.09 W of where it started — which drift and coincidence do not do.

What that means day to day

  • About 2.4% of a 60.6 Wh pack per hour, holding a static image.
  • Whole-machine runtime goes 9.1 h → 11.6 h, so roughly 21% of available working time.
  • For scale, a stock Framework 13 LCD is quoted around 1.8 W with the backlight at 0% and 4.3 W at 100% (community figures, different OS, order-of-magnitude). So this is cheaper than a backlight you would actually read by, and in the same league as a dimmed one.

The catch

The panel is bistable: once the image is on the glass it stays there with no current at all. The 1.4 W is the controller board and FPGA sitting idle, holding that picture. Refreshing the screen drives the panel drivers hard for seconds and costs more energy than holding does.

So 1.4 W is the number for a reader workflow — long static documents, an occasional page turn — which is exactly where e-paper is meant to win. It is not the number for scrolling.

A trap, if you measure this yourself

Unplug the cable and the draw does not step down. It decays from about 6.9 W to 5.1 W over roughly a minute, as the machine relaxes into the new power profile:

Averaging the whole window naively reports −1.09 W and understates the effect by about a third. A 30-second window would have been badly wrong.

Measure it yourself

git clone https://github.com/cittadhammo/eink-power
cd eink-power
systemd-inhibit --what=idle:sleep --why="eink power test" \
  ./bin/einkpower.py unplug-test

It samples battery power at 1 Hz from the hardware monitor, walks you through the phases, beeps when each window closes, and prints the table above. A verify subcommand first checks the current sensor against the battery’s own coulomb counter, so you know the wattage figures are real before you believe them.

Full method, the things that can and cannot be measured on a Linux laptop, and the open questions: github.com/cittadhammo/eink-power. Not yet measured: the cost of a refresh, and a head-to-head against the built-in LCD on this machine.
